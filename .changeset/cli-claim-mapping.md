`auth-method sso enable` and `setup` now map provider claims to user-schema properties. The standard OpenID Connect claims whose names match a property of the schema (`email`, `givenName`, `familyName`, `picture`, and the rest of the profile, email and phone claims whose scope the connection requests) are mapped by default, for Google and custom connections alike. To fill another property, repeat `--claim <property>=<claim>` (`--sso-claim` on `setup`); a claim the provider returns as a number, integer or boolean says so after a colon, as in `--claim staffNumber=employee_id:integer`. `--claim-scope` adds the scopes the claims need, and `--verified-claim <property>=<claim>` or `<property>=true` says how a unique property is verified. A claim you give replaces the default for its property (`--claim email=mail`), and a replaced default is no longer verified until `--verified-claim` says how. A claim fills one property and a property takes one claim, a claim's type has to fit the property's (a string cannot fill an integer), and a property the schema does not define is refused, all before a secret is asked for or a file written. Interactively, with no claim flag given, both commands show what is mapped and keep asking for more claims, the property to fill, the scope and the verification, until the person continues. A connection file that already exists keeps its own mapping and the command warns that the claims were not applied. `--json` reports the mapping, the verification and the scopes of a connection it creates.
