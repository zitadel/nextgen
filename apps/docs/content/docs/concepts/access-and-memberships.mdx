---
title: Access and memberships
description: How people get project roles through owning teams, grants and invitations, and how team membership differs from user lifecycle ownership.
---

Two kinds of membership sit side by side in Zitadel. **Project membership** is who can administer or read a [project](/docs/concepts/project-team-user#project). **Team membership** is which [users](/docs/concepts/project-team-user#user) belong to a [team](/docs/concepts/project-team-user#team) inside a project. [Manage project access](/docs/guides/manage-project-access) shows the commands.

## Project access

Console accounts and the teams that own customer projects live in a reserved **platform project**. A customer project must be [claimed](/docs/concepts/project-team-user#claim) before it can invite collaborators or create grants when the server has a platform project. Claim attaches an owning team. Today every active member of that team inherits `admin` on the project.

A project role is one of `viewer`, `editor`, or `admin`. Roles are additive: `admin` includes `editor`, and `editor` includes `viewer`. The strongest role wins when several paths apply. Roles are project-scoped, not per origin or environment.

Besides the owning team, access comes from [grants](/docs/reference/api/grants) and [invitations](/docs/reference/api/invitations). A grant binds a user or a team on the platform project to a role. An invitation is a 72-hour shareable link that offers a role to whoever opens it while signed in; you share the link out of band. Effective members are the union of these paths. A deployment can also configure platform operators with standing `admin` on every project; they are not listed as project members.

## Team memberships vs. lifecycle ownership

A team membership is a roster entry inside a project: whether a user belongs to that team right now. [User lifecycle ownership](/docs/concepts/project-team-user#user) is separate: which team may deprovision the user. Belonging to a team does not give that team lifecycle control over a self-owned user.

Today a membership is created when you create a user with a team, or when the platform bootstraps a personal team for a console account. There is no standalone endpoint yet to add or remove an existing user from a team, or to invite someone into a team.
