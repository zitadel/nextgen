---
title: Policies
description: The rules Zitadel checks before an operation runs, what applies without them, and how to test them.
---

A policy holds the rules Zitadel checks before an operation runs. A project has
at most one policy per operation. `zitadel setup` writes two:

- The password policy, for `auth.factor.password.set`, the operation that sets
  a password. It decides how long a new password must be.
  [Set the password policy](/docs/guides/password-policy) has its settings.
- The sign-up policy, for `user.create`, checked before a sign-up in your login
  creates a user. It names the challenge a sign-up must solve.

A policy is a file in your repository, `.zitadel/policies/<operation>.json`,
like your schemas and flows ([Configuration as code](/docs/concepts/repo-config)).

## Defaults

Every setting of a policy is optional, and a setting left out is off. What
Zitadel does without a setting depends on the operation:

- The password policy has no defaults: what the file sets is what applies. With
  no file, or with `"config": {}`, only Zitadel's fixed limits apply: a
  password may not be empty or longer than 64 characters.
- The sign-up policy's protection applies with or without its file. Deleting it
  changes nothing; the file shows the protection beside your other settings.

## Releases

A policy is one of the resources a release pins, like your schemas and flows. A
changed file becomes a new revision of the policy when you build a release, and
the change applies once that release is deployed.
[Releases and deployments](/docs/concepts/releases-and-deployments) explains
which release a request is checked against, and what a rollback brings back.

## Tests

A policy test is a file of cases beside the policy,
`.zitadel/policies/<operation>.test.json`. Each case is an input to the
operation and the decision you expect. Zitadel evaluates each case with the
operation's own check, exactly as a real call would be checked, and stores and
changes nothing.

A test runs against your local policy files, or against the revisions a release
pins. It never becomes part of a release, and `zitadel deploy` does not run it:
run `zitadel policy test` yourself before you deploy, or in CI.
[Test a policy](/docs/guides/test-policies) shows how.
